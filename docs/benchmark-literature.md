# Racing Lab benchmark literature review

Research date: 11 October 2026. See the [draft benchmark protocol](benchmark-protocol.md) for proposed experiments and implementation tasks.

Scope update: the active first stage is online RL from scratch, without human or expert-controller datasets. Use the [online RL paper audit](online-rl-paper-audit.md) for the current shortlist, source revisions and reproduction gaps. The broader demonstration, IO/IRL and offline proposals below are deferred context, pending a separate research decision.

Racing Lab can support a benchmark for learning fast, valid driving from a small number of good but imperfect demonstrations. Published work provides racing protocols and candidate algorithms to adapt. The results below concern their original environments; performance in Racing Lab remains an empirical question.

The central comparison should distinguish supervised imitation, reward or cost inference, and improvement through reinforcement learning. Methods receive different information: recorded actions, quality labels, ranked trajectories, task rewards, new expert corrections, simulator interactions, or a known dynamics model. Those differences determine which comparisons are fair.

## Published racing settings

| Paper and publication | Setting and inputs | Methods evaluated | Implication for Racing Lab |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [O'Kelly et al. F1TENTH An Open Source Evaluation Environment for Continuous Control and Reinforcement Learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v123/o-kelly20a.html), 2020 proceedings | Scaled autonomous racing with numerical sensors and optional vision. Separate known-map time trials, mapless driving, and competitive racing. | Pure pursuit, MPC tuning with CMA-ES, Follow-the-Gap, DAgger, and several planning methods across the tasks. | Start with a single-car time trial. Specify map access and collision rules before extending to opponents. |
| [Evans et al. Unifying F1TENTH Autonomous Racing Survey Methods and Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18558v2), 2024 | Four scaled track maps; LiDAR and speed for the learned policy, privileged map and pose for classical control. | Trajectory optimization with tracking, model predictive contouring control (MPCC), Follow-the-Gap, and TD3 in the reported learning experiments. | Measures successful-lap time, completion, and progress before failure. Its 100 Hz dynamics and normally 25 Hz planning show why control frequency belongs in the protocol; the training script overrides this rate (see the [source audit](online-rl-paper-audit.md)). [Author code](https://github.com/BDEvan5/f1tenth_benchmarks). |
| [Sun et al. A Benchmark Comparison of Imitation Learning Based Control Policies for Autonomous Racing](https://shuoyang2000.github.io/resources/papers/IV_Sun_2023.pdf), IV 2023 | F1TENTH simulation and physical transfer. Learner gets LiDAR; the pure-pursuit expert also sees position and heading. Actions are target speed and steering. | BC, DAgger, HG-DAgger, EIL, PPO, and each imitation method followed by PPO. | Compares label efficiency and closed-loop driving, including an unseen map. Interactive imitation uses fresh expert labels, so it needs a separate information budget. [Author code](https://github.com/mlab-upenn/f1tenth_il). |
| [Herman et al. Learn to Race A Multimodal Control Environment for Autonomous Racing](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.11575v3), ICCV 2021 | Richer racing simulator with track models, camera observations and numerical vehicle telemetry. Experiments include a vision-only policy. | Human, random control, iLQR-based MPC, and SAC. | Uses training and test tracks, three-lap tasks, and a test adaptation allowance. Report zero-shot testing separately from adaptation. Completion, boundary admissibility, speed and smoothness are useful metrics. |
| [Remonda et al. Formula RL Deep Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Racing using Telemetry Data](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.11106), arXiv 2021 | TORCS numerical telemetry; continuous steering, throttle and brake, with 200 ms decisions. | Ten DDPG variants using observation histories, an LSTM critic, multistep targets and prioritized replay; handcrafted/genetic controller references. | A close match to our numerical observations and three controls. Evaluates lap time and damage on several tracks. Its general-model selection consults unseen-track performance; our final test tracks must remain outside checkpoint selection. |
| [Koirala and Fleming F1tenth Autonomous Racing With Offline Reinforcement Learning Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04198v1), arXiv 2024 | Reward-labeled waypoint-controller data, 100 episodes per training track. Policies use LiDAR, speed and method-dependent history. | IQL, TD3+BC, CQL, AWAC, PLAS, Decision Transformer, Diffusion Policy, and return-conditioned tree policies. | Direct precedent for offline RL, including unseen tracks and friction shifts. This uses substantially more controller data than our initial human dataset. History lengths and dataset sizes must accompany comparisons. |
| [Czechmanowski et al. On learning racing policies with reinforcement learning](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02420v2), IROS 2025 | Numerical vehicle, wheel and actuator state plus upcoming road geometry; identified dynamics; physical RC testing on one track. | PPO, MPC, and a human RC driver. | Supports numerical observations and actuator modeling. Same-track physical performance does not establish unseen-track generalization. [Author project](https://grzegorzczput.github.io/rl-racing/). |
| [Lee et al. SAC(lambda) Efficient Reinforcement Learning for Sparse Reward Autonomous Car Racing Using Imperfect Demonstrations](https://www.inrol.snu.ac.kr/_files/ugd/0c0760_47fe45bbcaad4d68b8c2d35eb894c131.pdf), IROS 2025 | Assetto Corsa, Ferrari 458 GT2, Monza and Silverstone1967. Numerical inputs; imperfect bicycle-model MPC demonstrations, roughly 30 minutes per track, followed by online learning. | SAC variants and the proposed discriminator-assisted SAC method with positive-unlabeled learning. | Particularly relevant to improving over imperfect demonstrations. These demonstrations come from a controller, rather than a human. Compare ordinary SAC, replay-seeded SAC and SAC+BC before attempting the full method. [Project](https://heesungsung.github.io/AC-RLRacer/), [code](https://github.com/INRoL-AD/ACRacer). |
| [Heidersberger et al. LOPAL Local Performance Aware Active Learning from Imperfect Demonstrations](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16888v1), RA-L 2026 | Fixed 2D Gym racing track; 15 imperfect keyboard-driven human demonstrations containing position trajectories, phase and local quality. | GMM/GMR trajectory learning, local-quality pruning, quality-conditioned regression with interpolation, TLFSD, and RLPD. | Very close to our human-data motivation. Its racing task targets fewer off-road penalties through trajectory generation; it is not a demonstration of minimum-time F1 control. Local corner/segment labels are a useful extension. [Project](https://jheiders.github.io/LOPAL/). |

Two additional interfaces help position the task. The [TORCS competition manual](https://computerscience.missouristate.edu/SAIL/_Files/Simulated-Car-Racing-Championship-Competition-Software-Manual.pdf) defines numerical track, vehicle and opponent sensors. [Gymnasium CarRacing](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/box2d/car_racing/) instead uses RGB observations and tile-coverage rewards. Racing Lab's numerical observations and lap-time objective should be stated explicitly.

The visual [DeepRacing study](https://www.madhurbehl.com/newpubs/weiss2020deepracing2.pdf) compares PilotNet, CNN-LSTM, waypoint and Bezier trajectory prediction in the F1 2019 game. Its useful lesson is to measure closed-loop driving: action prediction error alone does not establish successful racing.

## Forward optimization references

| Reference | Approach | Information and adaptation required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Liniger et al. Optimization Based Autonomous Racing of 1 to 43 Scale RC Cars](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.07300), journal 2015 | Trajectory planning with tracking MPC and MPCC; numerical vehicle state, known track, nonlinear tire model. | A classical controller reference requires a model, track geometry, constraints and a shared mapping to our steering/throttle/brake controls. MPCC performs forward optimization; it does not infer a human's objective. |
| [Rosolia et al. Autonomous Racing using Learning Model Predictive Control](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.06534), ACC 2017 | Learning MPC builds a sampled safe set and terminal cost from previous successful laps, with system identification. | Suitable for a later repeated-lap improvement track. Prior trajectories and model/solver access must be declared. Published feasibility and improvement claims depend on its assumptions and setup. |

Racing Lab's existing geometric controller is a conservative smoke-test reference. It sees the full track and simulator state. It is not a globally optimal expert or a minimum-time solution.

## Inverse optimization and reward learning

These methods provide different answers to the question of what a demonstrator is optimizing. Their original papers are general control or learning references unless a racing setting is specified above.

| Primary reference | Method | Fit and limits for this benchmark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Abbeel and Ng Apprenticeship Learning via Inverse Reinforcement Learning](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ang/papers/icml04-apprentice.pdf), ICML 2004 | Match demonstrated feature expectations through repeated forward policy optimization. | An interpretable linear-feature baseline. Forward-solver approximation and rollout cost matter. Matching feature expectations does not uniquely recover the reward. |
| [Ziebart et al. Maximum Entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bziebart/publications/maximum-entropy-inverse-reinforcement-learning.html), AAAI 2008 | Fit a probability distribution over trajectories using reward features and an entropy principle. | Handles variability probabilistically; route-choice experiments are relevant to driving. Continuous racing requires an approximation to planning and the trajectory distribution. |
| [Ziebart et al. Modeling Interaction via the Principle of Maximum Causal Entropy](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bziebart/publications/maximum-causal-entropy.pdf), ICML 2010 | Causal entropy for sequential feedback decisions. | A suitable theoretical basis for soft sequential control. A sampled or approximate implementation must describe its forward optimizer and cannot claim exact likelihoods automatically. |
| [Levine and Koltun Continuous Inverse Optimal Control with Locally Optimal Examples](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2012/papers/43.pdf), ICML 2012 | Local probabilistic approximation around demonstrations; assumes local rather than global optimality. | Potentially useful for smooth fixed-horizon driving segments. Human "good" labels do not establish local optimality. Barrier contacts and gearbox mode changes complicate derivatives. |
| [Keshavarz et al. Imputing a Convex Objective Function](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/imputed_objective_msc.pdf), 2011 | Infer objective coefficients by minimizing residuals of Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) optimality conditions for a convex forward problem. | Strong structured IO reference for a separate convex control surrogate. Racing Lab's full hybrid nonlinear simulator is not a convex optimization problem. Fix the objective scale to exclude trivial solutions. |
| [Aswani et al. Inverse Optimization with Noisy Data](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.2017.1705), Operations Research 2018 | Inverse estimation for noisy observations of decisions from convex optimization models. | Separate measurement noise from deliberately suboptimal actions. Statistical guarantees depend on model and regularity assumptions. |
| [Mohajerin Esfahani et al. Data Driven Inverse Optimization with Imperfect Information](https://mohajerinesfahani.github.io/Publications/journal/2015/InvOpt.pdf), Mathematical Programming 2018 | Suboptimality losses and distributionally robust inverse optimization under imperfect information. | Especially relevant to bounded rationality and imperfect demonstrations. Tractable reformulations depend on the forward problem's structure; they are not drop-in full-race solvers. |
| [Menner and Zeilinger Maximum Likelihood Methods for Inverse Learning of Optimal Controllers](https://ifatwww.et.uni-magdeburg.de/ifac2020/media/pdfs/3143.pdf), IFAC 2020 | Likelihood-based inverse learning for finite-horizon nonlinear control, with relaxed optimality conditions. | A local inverse-control extension with known dynamics and an explicit noise model. Nonlinear KKT stationarity alone does not certify global optimality. |
| [Finn et al. Guided Cost Learning Deep Inverse Optimal Control via Policy Optimization](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/finn16.html), ICML 2016 | Sample-based MaxEnt cost learning coupled to policy optimization, including nonlinear costs. | Useful continuous-control reference when exact dynamics/planning are unavailable. Requires new rollouts and a specified sampling/optimization procedure. |
| [Fu et al. Learning Robust Rewards with Adversarial Inverse Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11248), ICLR 2018 | AIRL learns a reward through adversarial discrimination and policy optimization. | A standard scalable IRL comparator with simulator interaction. Reward transfer properties rely on its reward structure and assumptions; they are not an automatic guarantee for this partial observation. |
| [Brown et al. Extrapolating Beyond Suboptimal Demonstrations via Inverse Reinforcement Learning from Observations](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/brown19a.html), T-REX, ICML 2019 | Learn reward from approximately ranked trajectories, then optimize a policy under that reward. | Very suitable when the user can compare two imperfect runs. Several "good" labels do not specify their relative order. Pairwise labels are additional supervision. |
| [Brown et al. Better than Demonstrator Imitation Learning via Automatically Ranked Demonstrations](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/brown20a.html), D-REX, CoRL 2019 proceedings 2020 | Use a cloned policy and increasing perturbations to generate ranked rollouts, then learn reward and a policy. | Reduces manual ranking but adds interaction. Increasing noise is an assumption about expected degradation; individual noisy runs need not be worse. |
| [Chen et al. Learning from Suboptimal Demonstration via Self Supervised Reward Regression](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v155/chen21b.html), SSRR, CoRL 2020 proceedings 2021 | Bootstrap reward learning with AIRL and use noisy policy rollouts for reward regression. | A later, more expensive imperfect-demonstration comparator. Account for every rollout stage and evaluate reward quality separately from policy performance. |

Reward ambiguity is a substantive part of the task. [Ng et al.](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ang/papers/shaping-icml99.pdf) characterize policy-preserving reward shaping, while [Skalse and Abate](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370226000512), Artificial Intelligence 2026, analyze partial identifiability and misspecified behavioral models. A method can recover useful behavior without recovering unique numerical weights. Human-data experiments therefore need downstream driving and held-out preference evaluation; weight error belongs only in a controlled, identifiable synthetic experiment.

| BC; history/recurrent BC supported by [Mandlekar et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v164/mandlekar22a.html), CoRL 2021 proceedings 2022 | Supervised observation-to-action policy. History can address missing state. | Demonstrations; no task reward. The cited human-data experiments concern manipulation, not racing. | Core. | [DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a/ross11a.pdf), AISTATS 2011 | Aggregate expert corrections at learner-visited states. | New rollouts and fresh expert action queries. | [GAIL](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/cc7e2b878868cbae992d1fb743995d8f-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2016 | Match demonstrated state-action occupancy through adversarial learning. | Demonstrations and learner rollouts; no task reward. | Extension beside AIRL. | [IQ-Learn](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/210f760a89db30aa72ca258a3483cc7f-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2021 | Learn a soft Q-function representing policy and implicit reward. | Reward-free transitions; offline and online versions must be distinguished. | Offline imitation comparator. | [TD3+BC](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/a8166da05c5a094f7dc03724b41886e5-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2021 | Offline actor-critic with a cloning regularizer and observation normalization. | Fixed reward-labeled transitions. | Core offline RL. [Code](https://github.com/sfujim/TD3_BC). |
| [IQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06169), ICLR 2022 | Expectile value learning and advantage-weighted cloning. | Fixed reward-labeled transitions; optional online fine-tuning is a separate condition. | Core offline RL. IQL and reward-free IQ-Learn are different methods. [Code](https://github.com/ikostrikov/implicit_q_learning). |
| [CQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04779), NeurIPS 2020; [ReBRAC](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/26cce1e512793f2072fd27c391e04652-Abstract-Conference.html), NeurIPS 2023 | Conservative value estimation; strengthened TD3+BC design, respectively. | Fixed reward-labeled transitions. | Later strong offline RL checks. CQL has racing precedent above; the ReBRAC source concerns general offline benchmarks. [CQL code](https://github.com/aviralkumar2907/CQL), [ReBRAC code](https://github.com/tinkoff-ai/ReBRAC). |
| [SAC](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/haarnoja18b.html), ICML 2018; [algorithms and applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05905), 2018 | Off-policy continuous control with entropy regularization. | Simulator transitions and task reward. | Core online RL. Separate training from scratch, demonstration-seeded replay and BC initialization. |
| [PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347), 2017 | On-policy clipped policy optimization. | Simulator transitions and task reward. | Second online RL comparator and BC+PPO extension supported by the racing study above. |
| [AWAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09359), 2020 preprint revised 2021 | Advantage-weighted actor learning from offline data with online improvement. | Reward-labeled data plus an explicitly budgeted fine-tuning phase when used. | Later offline-to-online extension. [Author project](https://awacrl.github.io/). |
| [Wang et al. Learning to Weight Imperfect Demonstrations](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wang21aa.html), ICML 2021 | Weight imperfect demonstrations within GAIL without extensive oracle quality annotations. | Demonstrations and online learner rollouts. | Additional imperfect-data comparator if unlabeled mixed data becomes central; generic MuJoCo/Atari evidence. |

Official code is useful for checking algorithm details, but several original repositories target old Gym, Python and accelerator dependencies. Pin the port, hyperparameters and backend versions. A current [Stable-Baselines3](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/) or [imitation](https://imitation.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) implementation is a reimplementation choice, not proof that the original paper's results were reproduced.

## Benchmark design references

[D4RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07219) motivates fixed datasets with nonexpert behavior and limited coverage. [Minari](https://minari.farama.org/content/basic_usage/) offers a Gymnasium-compatible, episode-preserving dataset format; an optional export could improve interoperability without replacing the game's existing NPZ recordings.

[Agarwal et al. Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/f514cec81cb148559cf475e7426eed5e-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2021, motivates uncertainty intervals and robust aggregate reporting. Its [rliable implementation](https://github.com/google-research/rliable) is a useful reference. The independent units in our experiment are training seeds, sessions and tasks, rather than adjacent simulator frames.

## Deferred demonstration research scope

A later demonstration benchmark could ask whether an algorithm can learn valid driving from few imperfect runs, generalize to a held-out circuit family, and improve lap time under a declared information and interaction budget. Reward-recovery accuracy would be a separate task requiring synthetic hidden objectives or independently collected preferences. These proposals are outside the active online RL stage.

Start with BC and history BC, then add offline IQ-Learn, a structured IO comparator, ranked-trajectory reward learning, AIRL, reward-based IQL and TD3+BC, and online SAC/PPO. Use the geometric controller as a privileged reference. DAgger, SPRINQL, LOPAL, SAC(lambda), CQL/ReBRAC and MPC belong in explicitly scoped extensions rather than one undifferentiated leaderboard.

For the IO comparison, a candidate-trajectory suboptimality loss is a practical adaptation of inverse-optimization ideas. It is described as a proposed Racing Lab baseline in the [protocol](benchmark-protocol.md#inverse-optimization-comparison), not as a published algorithm already validated on this simulator. Convex KKT methods need a separate surrogate control task if their original assumptions are to hold.
